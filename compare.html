Why the model matters more than the number

A percentage fee scales with the salary. A flat fee doesn't.

The moment you hire above entry-level, a % fee stops being a discount and starts being a tax. On a $90,000 senior hire, a 20% fee costs more than double our flat rate — and at 35%, more than eight times as much. The math only gets worse as the role gets more senior, which is exactly when firms with % pricing are least likely to publish their number up front.

Beyond flat vs. percentage

Why a flat placement fee beats renting a seat

Placement isn't the only model out there. A large part of the market — monthly bundles and hourly marketplaces — prices the relationship differently. Here's the honest tradeoff.

Monthly bundles (they employ, one invoice)

Providers like TECLA ($4,500–$8,500/mo), Athyna ($4,500–$11,000/mo), and BEON ($7,000–$10,000/mo) roll salary, overhead, and their margin into one recurring invoice. Convenient — but you're renting the seat. Stop paying, and the relationship with that developer ends.

  • You pay: every month, indefinitely
  • You own: nothing — the vendor is the employer of record in practice
  • Best for: short-term or trial engagements where flexibility matters more than cost

Hourly marketplaces

Platforms like Lemon.io ($40–$90/hr, plus a reported ~$14,000 fee if you hire the contractor directly), Turing ($100–$200/hr, roughly a 50% markup over developer pay), and CloudDevs ($45–$75/hr) are built for fast, flexible sourcing — priced per hour, indefinitely.

  • You pay: an hourly markup, for as long as the engagement runs
  • You own: nothing without an extra buyout fee
  • Best for: short bursts of work, not a permanent team member
With Nearwork, you pay the flat fee once, and the hire is yours — full-time, on your payroll or ours through COR, with no ongoing markup and no per-hour tax. Renting a seat makes sense for a 3-week project. It stops making sense the day you want a real team member.

Common questions

Why doesn't this table include every nearshore staffing company?

Because most of them don't publish a price. We only compare against numbers we can point to on a public page — anything else would be a guess dressed up as a fact, and that's not a table we're willing to publish.

Is Nearwork always the cheapest option?

We're the lowest published flat fee for junior and mid-level roles, and tied with HireLATAM at the senior tier. We're not claiming to be the cheapest possible option in every scenario — we're claiming to be transparent about the one we charge.

Do any of these prices include payroll, benefits, or compliance?

No — the figures above are placement fees only, for sourcing and hiring the person. If you also need in-country payroll, compliance, and benefits administration, that's a separate service (Nearwork's COR starts at $99/contractor/month). Check whether a competitor's number includes that before comparing directly.
